#### Film/TV (201–212)

> **201. Groundhog Day** — *Film/TV*
> **Fact:** The 1993 Bill Murray film is set in Punxsutawney, PA, but not a single frame was shot there — it was filmed in Woodstock, Illinois, 61 miles northwest of Chicago. Crews built a fake "Welcome to Punxsutawney" sign and removed it after filming.
> **Bridges:** "Groundhog Day" entered Merriam-Webster's dictionary as a common noun meaning "a situation in which the same usually negative or monotonous experiences occur repeatedly" — a movie title that became standard English.
> **Sources:** Fox 32 Chicago, Silly America (Woodstock filming); Merriam-Webster (dictionary entry).
> **Fits:** regular
> **Staleness:** none

> **202. The Goonies** — *Film/TV*
> **Fact:** The 1985 Spielberg-produced treasure-hunt film was shot on location in Astoria, Oregon, with the real "Goondocks" neighborhood standing in for the kids' threatened homes.
> **Bridges:** Astoria is the oldest city in Oregon and the first permanent American settlement west of the Rockies — founded in 1811 as Fort Astoria by John Jacob Astor's fur-trading company.
> **Sources:** Oregon Encyclopedia, Travel Oregon (filming location); Wikipedia (Astoria, Oregon / Fort Astoria).
> **Fits:** regular
> **Staleness:** none

> **203. M\*A\*S\*H** — *Film/TV*
> **Fact:** The Korean War sitcom never filmed in Korea — every exterior, including the famous helicopter-flyover opening, was shot at the old 20th Century Fox Ranch (now Malibu Creek State Park), California, 1972–1983.
> **Bridges:** The same Fox Ranch backlot was used as the "ape village" set for the original 1968 *Planet of the Apes*.
> **Sources:** Malibu Creek State Park official site, Looper (M\*A\*S\*H filming); Corvid Sketcher, movie-locations.com (Planet of the Apes link).
> **Fits:** regular
> **Staleness:** none

> **294. Eugene Shoemaker** — *Records/Misc*

> **Bridges:** Co-discoverer of Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, whose 1994 collision with Jupiter was a major astronomy news event; namesake of Meteor Crater, Arizona.

> **300. Pluto** — *Records/Misc*

> **Sources:** Wikipedia's Pluto article (citing Venetia Burney's grandfather Falconer Madan and astronomer Herbert Hall Turner); Disney-history accounts dating to Ben Sharpsteen's own record of the timing.
