Deep Space — v2

Redesign per Ben's v1 notes. Stars are the hero — 3 size tiers, 5 twinkle groups on primes, per-star color and brightness. Skies are Sonora-structured: 5 night palettes as multi-stop vertical linear ramps, cross-fading on a 775s cycle mutually prime with the 344s 8-pane loop — 8 × 5 = 40 distinct looks that never re-sync. The bottom-left nebula is gone. Planet is half size, on 2 of 8 panes. The question staggers in word-by-word after each pan settles. Transform and opacity only, no requestAnimationFrame.

Round 1 · Question 1

The multiplication

8 panes × 5 palettes = 40 looks. Pane loop 344s (2³·43), palette loop 775s (5²·31) — coprime, never re-sync.

Periods

11·13·17·19·23s twinkle, 29·31·37·41·43s breathe/glint, drift 88·104·127·310·430·1560s.

Loop

344s — 8 panes, 39s flat hold + 4s glide each, wraps 8→1 via a cloned pane 9. Question re-staggers per pane.

Events

Shooting star 1/40–120s jittered ±40%, ~560px/s. GIF + sound bite 3–4/night, host-fired.

What to look at, and what it's proving

Watch from across the room. Calibration is a 55" panel at 15 feet. On a laptop the drift looks too slow and the grain too strong — correct, not a defect. Use 12× to preview the pane and palette cycles.

  • The multiplication is the point. The 344s pane cycle and the 775s palette cycle share no factor, so pane 3 under the ember sky and pane 3 under the teal sky are different rooms. 40 distinct looks from one asset set, zero extra art.
  • The sky is Sonora's structure, not Sonora's hues. Each palette is a multi-stop vertical linear ramp — a real color journey top to bottom — never a centered radial blob. That structural swap is why the bottom-left of v1 (the nebula smudge) is gone rather than shrunk: the smudge was a radial-blob problem, not a size problem.
  • Stars carry the world now. Three size tiers (dust / mid / bright), five twinkle groups on primes 11–23s, per-star base alpha and a 4-color temperature spread. Dust never animates — that keeps the animated-element count near 800 instead of 2000 on the stick. The bright tier runs hotter than the value law's 0.30–0.55 band on purpose: it's the hero tier, a few per pane, documented here as the sanctioned exception.
  • Every pane is nameable. River, planet, veil, cluster, asteroids, crescent, binaries, quiet. A pane that's just "more stars" is a wasted pane — pane 1's density is shaped into a river, and pane 8's sparseness sits under a dust lane, so even the extremes have a reason.
  • The wrap is a clone, not a rewind. Pane 9 is seeded with pane 1's exact RNG seed, so the 100%→0% keyframe jump lands on identical pixels. 8 flows into 1 with no backwards pan.
  • The question is typography, not a card. Words enter 45ms apart, DM Sans 500, after the pan settles; they leave fast and together when the camera moves. The scrim is a linear band behind the text only — a full-frame wash flattens the art to mush.
  • The shooting star tapers. Head-origin scaleX means the tail collapses into the head at death; a linear x run nested inside an ease-in y sag gives the arc without animating anything but transforms.

Craft notes honored from the lessons file: every soft glow lives on a border-radius:50% element (box-shadow on a square div is a blurred rectangle); every radial gradient reaches transparent inside its own box with real margin; blur values are static and graduated, never animated; the pane cycle is linear because eased CSS animations re-apply timing per keyframe segment; palette cross-fades are stacked-layer opacity, not animated color variables, because interpolating gradient colors repaints every frame and this layer has to survive 2.5 hours on a cheap stick.
