Ben: "what if we wanted the transition to arc a bit? just to add dimension and depth." A straight translate shows parallax on one axis; a curved camera path shows it on two. Same minimal world in all four stages (same seeds, same layers, same Sonora dusk ramp), same 820ms detent, same easing — the only variable is the arc. Verdict: ship RISE, at 0.8–1.0×

RISE is the one that earns the word "dimension" without spending anything to get it. The lift-and-settle puts parallax on both axes, the horizon stays level, and an upward arc matches what the beat means — moving on to the next thing. At 4–5% of stage height it registers as depth from 15 feet without ever displacing the question text's neighborhood enough to notice consciously. It also degrades gracefully: at 0.5× it is still a different animation from FLAT; BANK at 0.5× is just a wobble.

BANK gets irritating first. It tilts the horizon and every vertical edge in the frame, and it does so behind question text roughly 100 times a night for 2.5 hours. Rotation is the strongest vestibular cue on this list — people who never notice a translate notice a tilt — and a repeated small roll stops reading as "camera banking" and starts reading as "the TV is loose" somewhere around the third round. DIP is subtly wrong rather than irritating: the world sagging under the text carries a faint "drop/error" connotation, and swinging under a grounded desert horizon fights the scene's own gravity. FLAT is the control and the fallback — never wrong, never dimensional; it answers Ben's question with "no."
