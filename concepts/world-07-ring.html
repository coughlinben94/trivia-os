World 07 · the ring

The ring

Twelve stations of sky. Each slide is one station; a turn rotates to the next; twelve turns brings you back. Layers move different distances per turn — 480, 1920 and 2880px — and that difference is the depth. Every layer's period is exactly twelve turns, so a station always lands on the composition it was authored with. Each station has its own brightness target, so some are quiet and two or three are loud, instead of twelve panes that all read the same.

What changed from world-06. Five measured defects, five fixes.

Real parallax. world-06 wrote the same travel distance onto every layer, so nothing moved relative to anything else during the one second you were watching. Now far moves 480px per turn, mid 1920, near 2880 — a 1 : 4 : 6 ratio.

It cannot run out. world-06's strip ended after 13 turns and the rest of the night was blank sky. Each layer is now a cylinder of exactly twelve times its own surge, so all layers hit phase zero together on turn twelve, in integer arithmetic. mid moves exactly one frame per turn, which is what makes a station reliably the frame you authored.

Panes differ. A value arc gives each station a brightness target with one crest and one trough per turn. Most stations are quiet so two or three can be loud. world-06's twelve panes spanned 1.097× — effectively identical.

The sky is nearly bare. world-06's fixed corner haze contributed 84% as much light as all twelve panes of content, identically every time — which is the opposite of "each pane is a different place."

Reproducible. Every position, size and alpha comes from one seeded hash, so the world is identical on every load and any defect can be re-rendered on demand.
