360° — the torus camera

The viewer sits inside a 360° world; each slide is a 45° window; an advance rotates the camera 45° in a fixed direction, and eight advances return you exactly to slide 1 — a structural loop, not a strip with a hidden seam. The world is a torus: one tile per layer, repeating in both axes, so travel is just a direction vector and every direction wraps with zero special-casing. The engine is fixed; the only weekly variable is the direction of travel — one config line, or the picker below.

How the torus works. Fixed engine, one weekly knob.

The wrap: each layer is one tile of art repeated in both axes (stars 48×48cq, mid 64×64, fg 96×96). Travel per advance is exactly one-sixth of the tile period, so six advances are one whole tile in any direction and the wrap-modulo lands you where you started — left, right, up, down, diagonal, or a spiral (eight 45°-rotated vectors sum to zero, so even the spiral closes its loop). The direction is a single vector in two CSS variables; WEEK_DIRECTION at the top of the script is the one line that changes week to week. No new art, no new code paths.

Drift + surge: every layer drifts along the travel axis forever (linear, prime durations 331/337/347 s, speed scaled by depth), so the world never stops; the 1.2 s detent is a surge over that motion, one asymmetric ease-out on travel plus a perpendicular arc with a continuous-velocity crest. The question's exit rides ON the move and the new question enters at settle — one gesture. Drift is folded into the base offset at every settle and on a 20 s idle clock, which is what keeps a two-axis tiling small enough for a cheap HDMI stick.

The 45° window is a flat mapping, stated plainly: no spherical projection, no 3D. Rotation is sold by parallax between depths plus a static corner vignette that makes the frame read as a porthole. Real edge curvature needs per-pixel warp — wrong trade for the target hardware.
