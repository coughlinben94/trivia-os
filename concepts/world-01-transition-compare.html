Trivia OS · World 01 · Transition Compare · 2026-07-30

PAN vs REEL

Same world twice. Stage A is v2's lateral pan: 39s flat hold, 4s glide. Stage B is the View-Master reel Ben floated — "the world rotates in theory": the same 8 panes wrapped on a drum, advanced one 45° detent at a time in a fixed 500ms beat (40ms anticipation back-tick → the throw → overshoot settle), with real parallax during the detent (fg 8cqw · mid 4cqw · stars 1.1cqw · sky 0.55cqw). Content is seed-identical between stages so the only variable is the transition. transform/opacity only, no requestAnimationFrame. The Advance buttons are the point — fire them until you're sick of it; that's the test.

APAN

v2's behavior — long flat holds, 4s lateral glide, seamless 8→1 wrap via cloned pane 9.

BREEL

Same 8 panes on a rotating drum. One 500ms detent per advance: back-tick, throw, settle. Parallax layers sell the diorama.

The detent

TURN, 820ms: 90ms anticipation (−2°), the turn, 98ms settle. No cross-fade — the new facet sweeps over the old one. Toggle to SNAP to feel the old 500ms.

The parallax

During the detent: fg 8cqw · mid 4cqw · far 1.1cqw · sky 0.55cqw. Sky returns to rest — no accumulation.

Shared content

Same seeds, same 8 anchors, same 5-palette 775s sky, same event channel, same question stagger (45ms/word). Only the transition differs.

Cost per night

~100 advances: PAN spends ~6.7 min in transit (4s each). REEL spends ~50s (0.5s each).

Consultant verdict

1 · Which wins: the REEL. Three reasons, in order of weight. First, depth is a named requirement and the pan can't prove it. v2's pan moves the whole pane as one sheet — its depth is painted, never kinematic. The reel's parallax demonstrates depth 100 times a night; every advance is a small proof that the world has a front and a back. That is the Sonora feeling ("the depth piece") made structural. Second, the detent is a cue, not just a transition. In a loud bar at 15ft, a 500ms mechanical snap reads as "next question" from peripheral vision — it's a bell. The pan's 4s glide is beautiful but announces nothing; guests look up mid-glide and wait. Third, time. 100 advances × 4s is nearly 7 minutes of a 2.5-hour night spent in transit with no question on screen. The reel gives ~6 of those minutes back.

2 · Costs. Build: the pan exists (v2, zero marginal); the reel is ~200 lines of choreography, now written. Per-world authoring: the reel is actually cheaper — pan panes must tile edge-to-edge (every pane's right margin must plausibly continue into its neighbor, plus a cloned pane 9 for the wrap); reel panes never share an edge, so each is a free-standing diorama and the 8→1 wrap is free. The reel does demand a layer split (fg/mid/far) per pane — but the diorama brief wants that anyway. Runtime: comparable — the reel stacks 8 full-frame panes but hidden panes park their animations (animation-play-state:paused), and the detent itself is transform-only and brief. Risk: the reel's one real unknown is perspective/rotateY on a cheap HDMI stick's GPU. Untested. The fallback — drop rotateY, keep translate + scale + parallax — preserves ~90% of the feel and is a 10-line change.

3 · Does the mechanism survive 100 viewings? Honestly: yes, if it stays exactly this size, and the anticipation tick is the part to watch. Identical mechanical beats survive repetition the way a clock tick or a slide-projector clunk does — it's expressive flourishes that curdle. This detent is 500ms, quiet, and never varies, which is the right recipe. The risk sits in the 45ms back-tick: a direction reversal is the most attention-grabbing thing in motion design, and at 100 reps a too-big tick would read as a twitch. It's currently 3.5° / 1.6cqw — small on a laptop, but tune it on the actual TV and be ready to halve it or cut it. If anything gets removed after a live night, it's the tick, never the settle. Do not add sound to the detent; 100 clunks a night is a firing offense.

4 · The View-Master mask: built, then deleted. Ben's call, and it's the right one — "the view master concept is just the flow. i dont wanna see them as actual panes with white in the middle and such." The toy is the rhythm, not the lens housing. The consultant verdict had independently landed the same place: the double-lobe ate about a third of the frame at exactly the aspect ratio a 16:9 bar TV can least afford, narrowed the question measure, and put a permanent bright ring on screen all night. Gone from the file entirely, not toggled off.

4b · No seam, structurally. The sky is a stage-level sibling of the drum, not a child of any pane, so it's continuously visible underneath the whole detent — the rotation can never expose a gap, a divider, or a flash of background. The panes themselves carry no background of their own. The drumshade edge darkening was softened from .5/11% to .30/22% for the same reason: at the original values it read as a frame edge, which is precisely the thing this design must never show.

5 · Before the winner goes near real code:

  1. Stick test first. One evening with the real HDMI stick and TV: rotateY performance, tick size, overshoot size. Nothing else is worth tuning before that.
  2. Advance becomes host-driven. The detent should fire on the Supabase question event, not a timer — the mechanism doubles as the show's metronome, which is its whole value.
  3. Layer split becomes an authoring convention. Every world's pane spec declares each anchor's layer (fg/mid/far) up front, so parallax is free per world instead of retrofitted.
  4. Reduced-motion cross-fade needs a render check — it's implemented (scoped exception under the blanket freeze) but unverified.
  5. Keep the pan. Not as the advance — as the idle. A world that pans slowly during scoring breaks and detents on questions gets both temperaments for the price of both being built.

Build notes

  • Deviation from v2, documented in the header: both stages advance from one JS scheduler (chained setTimeout on natural boundaries — v2's own sanctioned exception) instead of v2's 344s keyframe loop, because on-demand Advance buttons are the point of this page. Cadence matches v2 exactly at 1×.
  • Per-segment easing is inside the keyframes on purpose. The lessons file's eased-animation trap is about predicting one property from another's keyframe%; here transform and opacity share stops within each animation and nothing cross-predicts, so per-segment timing functions are safe and give the detent its mechanical shape.
  • The reel's hidden panes pause their star animations — 8 stacked panes of twinkle would otherwise burn compositor time invisibly all night.
  • Stage B's sky-kick ends where it starts (0 → +0.22 → −0.55 → 0 cqw), so 100 advances accumulate no drift.
  • Stage A in reduced motion cuts instantly (transition killed by the freeze); only the reel carries the mandated cross-fade degrade.
  • Not rendered. This session has no browser. Per the lessons file, visual work is not done until someone renders it and looks — that pass is still owed, especially for the detent feel, the mask, and the rm cross-fade.
