World 06 · rotating panes

Rotating panes

A 360° view of the sky. Each slide is one pane; advancing turns the view to the next. Stars drift the whole time underneath, so a turn reads as a turn and not a cut. Every other pane holds something — a nebula, a supernova, a comet, a cluster, a dust lane — and the panes between them are open sky, so the features land. Twelve panes make a full turn. Text alternates purple and orange.

Time

How it works. Twelve panes, six features, one turn.

A pane is exactly one frame wide, and a turn slides the sky by exactly one pane. Twelve panes bring you back to where you started, so it is a real 360 rather than a strip with a hidden join.

The drift is the glue. Two star layers move continuously at different speeds, so the sky is never frozen and the turn lands on something already alive. Without it, a turn between two still frames reads as a cut — that was the diagnosis on the previous four attempts.

Features on even panes only. Six across the turn. If every pane had something, nothing would feel like an arrival; the open panes are what make the nebula land when it comes round.

No jolt, by construction. Drift runs on .rail, the turn runs on .surge, and they nest. There is no settle step, so there is no moment where two numbers have to agree — which is exactly what produced the pop before.
