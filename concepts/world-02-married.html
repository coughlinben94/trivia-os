Married — A’s world, B’s motion

Ben: “its almost like A and B need to be married. the objects arent staying on B like A, but B moves muchhhhh smoother.” So: no panes. One continuous layered world — fg, mid, starfield, sky — each strip tiling at its exact wrap period, and a camera that lands on 8 stations with world-01’s 820 ms TURN detent riding the arc study’s RISE path. Advance eight times and you are back at station 1 with no seam, and every planet you passed is the same planet coming around again. Themes cross-fade on their own clock, mutually prime with the station cycle.

Arc 1.00×

How the marriage works. One of each thing, ever.

Persistence: each layer is a single continuous strip whose content repeats at exactly 8× its per-station travel (fg 272cqw, mid 128cqw, stars 48cqw). The detent animates a mover; at animationend the base offset absorbs the travel modulo the wrap period in one task. Nothing is ever swapped, faded, or rebuilt — an object that leaves the frame is the same DOM node that returns seven stations later.

The arc: the RISE path from the arc study, on both axes of parallax — fg 34cqw / 1.0 Y, mid 16 / 0.48, stars 6 / 0.18, sky 0 / 0.07. Beat is v2 TURN’s: 90 ms anticipation, one asymmetric ease-out, 98 ms settle, still easing at 100%. Opacity constant throughout. Drag the Arc slider to scale the lift from flat to 2×.
