# Ben's Taste Profile — the steering document for fact-hunt, the grader, and the three format generators

> Built 2026-09-02 from a full read of the live `questions` table (1,579 regular · 141 bonus · 281 shiny · 84 swing · 48 PYL rows) by two independent Fable 5.1 reviewers (reports A and B, merged here), plus the 11 fact-hunt facts Ben has used and his keep/kill verdicts from the 2026-09-01 wave. Ben's own words that morning: *"i dont care about era. i focus on just plain old cool things. fandoms are the goals here."* — and, later that day, the correction: *"there has to be a genuine balance of cool trivia facts and fandoms."* The bank agrees (see well 14). Every hunter, every grader, every generator reads this before doing anything. `question-anatomy.md` is the companion (the moves); this file is the taste (the wells, the shapes, what counts as a bridge, what gets killed).

## 0. The one-page brief (every hunter and grader Reads this file; paste §0 verbatim when a prompt can't Read)

**A Ben fact is a word the bar already owns, reached by a road it hasn't walked, with a second door in.** The subject is famous. The dig is a detail — or a JOIN between two famous things — nobody at the table has noticed. It is NOT "an amazing thing nobody has heard of." *Cool = a thing everyone already loves, seen from an angle they never noticed.*

1. **Answer first.** Write the answer on line 1. If the table can't say it without being told, stop. (Phil LaMarr and Sterling Holloway died on this; Frank Oz lived.) One answer only — if two defensible answers exist, tighten the aim or flag it `name either`.
2. **Subject is something the bar already knows — a fandom OR a real-world thing.** The join test: would the resident fan already have connected these two things? If yes, it's the subject's lead sentence — keep digging; if no, it's fresh (Kirby = Jack Kirby, Tantive IV = Corvette class). Fandoms: The Office, Star Wars, Disney parks, comics, Potter/LOTR/GoT, Pokémon/Nintendo, Nicktoons, dad rock, country, 2000s pop, Tigers/MSU/"the four major sports," Frat Pack comedies, cryptids/haunted/true crime, myth, Michigan/Saginaw, toys, fast food, booze. Real world (well 14, `Cool-facts`, ~1 in 5 of the bank): Iwo Jima, Lewis and Clark, the Cold War, sloths, Tesla, Genghis Khan, Australia's fence, PT Barnum — famous subjects, not a forgotten treaty. **Genuine balance of the two, Ben's words.** What a real-world fact still needs: a §2 hinge (somebody did the math, an only/first, a name that's secretly something else, gross/dumb, a legend) and an answer the bar can say. A pop door helps; it is not required.
3. **The hinge is the fact.** A name that's secretly something else · a real thing that leaked into fiction or vice versa · an almost-happened · somebody did the math · an only/first/last · a word origin with a pop twin. "Also voiced X" is a résumé. A lawsuit is a lawsuit. "Actually that's false" is a corpse. Frank Oz (Yoda = Miss Piggy) is a résumé fact Ben KEPT; Sterling Holloway (also voiced Kaa) is one he killed — the shout separates them, not the shape.
4. **Bridges are doors, typed.** `name:` lives in another universe · `line:` a quote/lyric/SFX · `ask:` a character or celebrity who'd know · `mi:` Michigan/Saginaw/family · `hook:` a cold-open that's secretly a clue. Filmographies and category nouns are not bridges.
5. **Keep the legend.** "Legend states…" is a feature. A legend stays a legend with `[disputed]`: tag the doubt, deliver it as legend, never lead with the debunk. If the only interesting thing is that it's false, drop it — a debunk that makes the thing smaller is a corpse (§6).
6. **Gross is fine, grim is not.** Sloths dying mid-poop, bat bombs, Uranus MO, honey badger: yes. Murder trials, war wounds, a cat dying on its first mission: no.
7. **Not already mined.** Grep `/Users/bencoughlin/Projects/baynes-trivia/trivia-os/FACT-HUNT-BANK.txt` (one line per bank row, `id<TAB>answer<TAB>text`, written by the orchestrator each round) for the answer AND the hinge. Hootie's name origin was already a swing item; so was STP's; Frank Oz was already #686.
8. **Shape it like his notes page.** Answer, then 2–5 fragments of ≤15 words in his register, then typed doors, then `media:` if a clip/photo exists, then the five-word **shout** — what the bar yells at the reveal. The `Shout:` line is mandatory: no shout, no fact.
9. **Verified: two sources;** the primary work (the lyric, the scene, the game level) counts as one for detail-inside facts; never a listicle, never the video/thread that surfaced it. Two sources disagree → dead, or the dispute becomes `[disputed]`.

Hunters self-reject against 1–9, and against shape: a fact that fits no §3 shape — a generic pub-quiz fact any host could use — is a reject even if true and bridged. The one `edge` fact per hunter is the only exception; that is all `edge` waives. The grader's binary bounces are 1, 7, 9 and lawsuit-as-the-fact (§6); everything else is a score input.

## 3. The shapes (how a Ben question is built — the hunter returns facts that fit one)

- **The answer:** one word or one proper noun the room owns — an animal (hippos, goat, sheep, cougars, camels, rhino, bison, pangolin, puffin, donkey, otters, sturgeon, bats, ostrich, bull, wolverine, lynx, muskrat), a place, a title, a brand, a character, a number. When it's a person, the bar can say the name (Pedro Martinez, Tina Fey, Bob Ross). **The obscurity lives in the road, never the destination:** "I knew the answer and I did not know THAT about it."
- **Detail-inside-a-famous-thing** (~40% of the bank). Levee (American Pie), Courtyard (Hotel California), Sauna (Frozen), Quack (Mighty Ducks), Flounder (SpongeBob), Lucille (Walking Dead), Marley (Home Alone), Turnip (Mario 2), Maurice (Friends), Arcade (Nickelback), Panther (Anchorman), Toga, Potions. Two-thirds of a Ben question's freshness is a JOIN the resident fan hasn't made — Kirby=Jack Kirby is the lead sentence of Kirby's article and still a 10/10, because nobody at the table has joined the two.
- **Name-with-a-second-life** — see §2.1. Stacking is the norm: Fleetwood = Elvis's Cadillac + half a band + a golfer; Marley = Scrooge's partner + a Labrador + a reggae legend; White Horse = oldest tavern + Stapleton + Gandalf's ride; Aurora = two cities + a goddess + Sleeping Beauty; Mad Hatter = Carroll + Theophilus Carter + a Disneyland storefront + a Batman villain; Demi = Starbucks cup + demigods + Demi Moore. The bridge is frequently a pun on the word itself (Squib, Caper, Plum, Spurs, Parlay, Lead, Cannonball, Van Dyke).
- **Lyric / quote / scene detail.** "If you're Tracy Chapman, and you happen to have a Fast Car, you work at what kind of establishment?" → Supermarket. ~25 of these; zero "fact" content; pure fandom. Also "Scenario:" / "Picture this…" second-person openers. The primary work is its own proof.
- **You-could-ask-[character] bridge.** Chandler Bing → Tulsa; Ken Hotate → Matchbox 20; Rupert Holmes → Cape; Scott Tenorman → Radiohead; Bobby Newport's godfather → Liechtenstein; Ronald Ulysses Swanson → Willie Nelson; Lance Parrish → Parish. The witness IS the pun.
- **Hook that is secretly a clue.** An unattributed line/SFX from a DIFFERENT property that contains the answer: "Ow! My patella!" → Wounded Knee; "*FLASH*" → Men in Black; "Th-th-th-the first supermarket…" → Piggly Wiggly; "I don't like sand…" → Corsica; "Leedle leedle lee!" → Flying Dutchman; "Stayin' alive" → pulsar; "97X, Bam!" → Hoffman; "Arg!!!" → Plunder; "Get a load of this!" → Robotnik. ~40% of questions open with one. A fact that hands the hunter a usable hook line is worth double — that is the `hook:` door.
- **Three-role actor riddle.** "Played an arrogant entrepreneur in 2004, an inventing entrepreneur in 2006, an acting entrepreneur in 2008" → Ben Stiller.
- **According-to-[list] record-book puzzle** and **four-major-sports logic.** "Only one team in the four majors whose name ends in a vowel" → Avalanche; "the only surname shared by two Best Actor winners" → Hoffman.
- **Name-origin.** Foo Fighters, REO Speedwagon, Doom, Steely Dan, Bad Company, Devo, Supertramp, Toad the Wet Sprocket, Maverick, Cracker Barrel, Chuck E. Cheese, the Guardians, the Lakers, Moxie.
- **"Heard here / seen here."** Audio and visual props are used constantly — a fact with a natural clip or photo is worth more (`media:`).
- **Michigan garnish.** Any fact plus one Michigan tie is a better fact: Corvette (Tantive IV + Flint), Idlewild (JFK airport + "Black Vegas"), Sheboygan (Home Alone polka + Michigan town), Kalamazoo (Gibson + Hard Rock).
- **Topical / in-memoriam.** "RIP to an absolute legend, Bill Rasmussen…" → OJ Simpson via ESPN's top-25. The death is the door; the work is the route; the death is never the punchline.

**Register:** first person, bar-buddy, unpolished, fast. Typos are Ben, not artifacts. Opinions ("my top-rated film Prisoners," "my dad's favorite movie," "Does Ben have a type?!?"). Lowbrow is fine ("It really doesn't give a shit…", "Dirty Hoe!", "Suck on that!"). He roasts Ohio, Nickelback, the Kardashians, his dad, himself; plugs the bar; cites his own past questions. Ellipses are the signature; em-dashes are not. Numbers are texture, not the point. **Era is irrelevant** — a 1937 Disney detail and a 2025 viral clip sit in the same round.

## 4. What counts as a bridge (the rule the 2026-08 hunters broke)

A bridge is a **second door into the same answer from a different fandom**, typed:
- **`name:`** the answer word/name living in a second universe — "shares its name with…";
- **`line:`** a quotable line, lyric, sound effect or catchphrase from the answer's own culture;
- **`ask:`** a character or celebrity who "would know" — the you-could-ask move;
- **`mi:`** a Michigan / Saginaw / family / Cedar Point / Tigers / MSU tie;
- **`hook:`** a cold-open from a DIFFERENT property that secretly contains the answer (the single most valuable thing a hunter can hand Ben; it is not "drafting a question").

**Not a bridge:** another fact about the same subject; a filmography ("bridges = Tootsie, Dog Day Afternoon"); a category noun ("real-estate market data," "the commercial pet-food industry," "animal-welfare oversight history," "forensic ballistics"); a restatement of the setup. The 2026-08 output had 61% single-bridge rows and most were one of these. Zero typed doors = not a Ben fact yet.
