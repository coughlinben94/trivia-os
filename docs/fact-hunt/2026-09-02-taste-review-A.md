## 2. The SHAPE of a Ben question

- **Register:** a fan talking to fans. First person, present tense, opinions ("my top-rated film Prisoners" #444, "my dad's favorite movie" #1301, "Does Ben have a type?!?" #2058, "I consulted at River Club" #1217, "one of my personal favorite bands" #220). ~12% first-person; the fact-hunter output is 3%.
- **The hook** is the true signature: an unattributed line from a DIFFERENT pop-culture property that secretly contains the answer or a clue. "Ow! My patella!" → Wounded Knee (#365). "*FLASH*" → Men in Black (#623). "Th-th-th-the first supermarket…" (Porky stutter) → Piggly Wiggly (#1621). "I don't like sand…" → Corsica (#71, Anakin). "Leedle leedle lee!" → Flying Dutchman (#364). "Stayin alive" → pulsar (#69). "Whistle whistle*" → Steamboat (#344, Willie). "You must absolve!" → White glove (#626). "97X, Bam!" → Hoffman (#852). The rule: quote the answer's culture in its own voice, never explain it.
- **Where the obscurity lives:** INSIDE a familiar thing. "In the film X, character Y does Z — what is Z?" (Levee #262, Courtyard #266, Sauna #673, Quack #237, Flounder #435, Lucille #438, Marley #555, Turnip #1965, Maurice #1764, Arcade #631, Panther #1200, Toga #1551, Potions #1555). The subject is famous; the detail is the dig. This is 40%+ of the bank and ~0% of the fact-hunter's output, which digs for obscure SUBJECTS (Hans Island, Jackson's cheese, Colosseum naming).
- **The answer:** usually one word or a name everyone can say. Very often a WORD WITH A SECOND LIFE — the same string in two universes: Colossus (#127), Kirby (#236), Creed (#132), Squib (#204), Echo (#341), Cerberus (#329), Jericho (#659), Sirius (#1313), Kowloon (#1804), Osprey (#1493), Vixen (#1291), Wizard/Ranger (#1967), Druid (#1894), Prestige (#1930), Plunder (#2626), Scarecrow (#2625), Paramount (#2647), Herman (#2648), Vector (#2645), Cosmo (#2135), Jumpman (#2060). Tri Bond and "We're not so different" are the same instinct scaled up.
- **The bridge verbs:** "shares its name with…" (the workhorse, ~15%); "you could ask [character]…" / "[character] may know" (Chandler Bing → Tulsa #1848; Ken Hotate → Matchbox 20 #961; Rupert Holmes → Cape #1772; Scott Tenorman → Radiohead #2630; Bobby Newport's godfather → Liechtenstein #309; Ronald Ulysses Swanson → Willie Nelson #923); "If you're [artist], and you're [lyric]…" (~25 questions, pure lyric lookup, zero "fact"). Bridges are pop-culture-to-pop-culture, rarely pop-culture-to-history.
- **Three-role actor riddles** ("played X in 1994, Y in 2002, Z in 2017" #1748, #1392, #1218, #1884, swing #1351) and **"only one in the 4 majors / RRHOF / Oscars / Billboard" record-book puzzles** are recurring templates.
- **The wink:** last clause, ellipsis, a pun or a second pop reference ("Don't go too fast, though, we wouldn't want you to turn to plaid…" #921; "PTTTTNNNNNNGGGGG!" #188; "Stick to baseball, bud, you're no Jerry Garcia…" #620).
- **Numbers** are texture, rarely load-bearing. Years appear; they're not the point.
- **Humor register:** PG-13, groaners, mild profanity, roast energy, wordplay-as-hint. "Dirty Hoe!" (#515), "Shitters full" (#1269), "white girl wasted" (#1873).

## 7. USED vs KILLED — what the hunter gets wrong

The 11 used: Renegade (one word, three universes: Secret Service, Jeep, Styx, Jay-Z), Butterfly Effect (term → Kutcher film), Patton → became "Plunder" (#2626: the operation NAME with a definition bridge), 99 Luftballons (band-name/song origin — a swing concept), Point Nemo (name → Verne), Ig Nobel bats (his own listed source), Superman ice cream (Michigan), Candy Land (toy origin), Detroit pizza (Michigan), Moxie → #2128 (brand → dictionary word), Gretzky 99 (sports name-origin; the Robin Thicke bridge was already in the bank #1740).

Kept 9/1: four band-name origins (Bad Company, Toad the Wet Sprocket, Supertramp, Devo), Frank Oz (voice-actor crossover), Duracell bunny (mascot lore), 555 (Hollywood convention), Low Rider (needle-drop: Dazed and Confused + George Lopez), Danny Trejo Spy Kids, UMich Apollo 15, St Valentine's/Michigan, Willie Mays only, I Will Survive/Disco Demolition, Coco Chanel codename, Littlefeather.

Killed 9/1: murder-for-hire trial (Lyin' Eyes), CIA Acoustic Kitty, Charles Durning war wounds, Pacino Ponzi/Razzie, AT-AT lawsuit, "eBay Pez story is fake," "Shepard's golf shot was short," Ig Nobel gas-mask bra, Nain Rouge legend is post-1880, Lasseter/Young Sherlock, Phil LaMarr, Sterling Holloway.

What separates them: **used/kept = the fact hands him a NAME or an ORIGIN he can bridge into pop culture (word-with-a-second-life, band/brand/toy/mascot origin, actor-crossover, Michigan). Killed = grim (murder, war wounds, dead cat), legal/lawsuit, "actually less cool than you thought" debunks, or a fact whose only bridge is a category label.** Also killed: voice-actor crossovers when the answer is a name nobody at the bar can say (Phil LaMarr, Sterling Holloway) — familiar destination failed.

So the hunter's five systematic errors:
1. Digs for obscure SUBJECTS instead of deep details INSIDE famous things.
2. Bridges are "another fact about the subject" or a category noun, not a second route to the answer WORD.
3. Answers are the subject of the fact (Whitney Houston, The Godfather) instead of a word/name that clicks.
4. Allocates by a flat domain wheel (science 8%, etymology 8%, history 13%) against a bank that is ~75% film/TV/music/games/comics/sports-as-pop-culture.
5. No hook material, no lyric/quote material, no topical lane, no "according to [list]" lane, no Michigan-garnish hunt.

- **Add a bridge-shape gate at merge:** a bridge counts only if it is (a) the answer word/name living in a second universe, (b) a quotable line/lyric/SFX from the answer's own culture usable as a hook, (c) a character who "would know" (the you-could-ask move), or (d) a Michigan/Saginaw/family tie. "More about the subject" and category nouns ("real-estate market data") do not count. Zero real bridges = bounce.
- **Add lanes** that don't exist today: (1) **lyric/quote-detail** — a detail inside a song or scene everyone knows; (2) **topical** — this week's deaths, releases, anniversaries, award classes, sports moments, tagged to expire; (3) **according-to-[list]** — Rolling Stone/IMDB/Guinness/Forbes/People/Toy HOF/RRHOF/Billboard record-book onlys/firsts/mosts; (4) **name-origin** — band/brand/toy/mascot/team/character/place name origins (4 of 15 kept on 9/1 were this); (5) **Michigan garnish** — for any fact, hunt a Michigan/Saginaw/Cedar Point/Tigers/brewery tie before returning it.
