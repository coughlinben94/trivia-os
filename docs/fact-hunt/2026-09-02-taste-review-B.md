# Review B — How Ben writes, what he writes about, and why the fact-hunter misses

Corpus read in full: bank_regular (1,400+), bank_bonus, bank_shiny, bank_swing, bank_pyl, bank_other, facthunt_used_and_killed. Sections 1–7 were written before opening any prompt file. Section 8 was written after.

Short version: Ben's question is not "a fact about X." It is **a familiar answer reached by an unfamiliar road, with a hinge** — a word, name, number, or mascot that lives in two fandoms at once. The fact-hunter is delivering facts *about* his fandoms (production trivia, lawsuits, résumés, debunks) instead of hinges *inside* them. That is why 11 of ~600 landed.

## 2. The SHAPE of a Ben question

### The answer
- Almost always **one word or one proper noun**: an animal, a place, a title, a brand, a character, a number. Animals alone are a huge share (hippos, goat, sheep, cougars, camels, alpaca, rhino, bison, pangolin, puffin, donkey, otters, elephant, sturgeon, bats, ostrich, bull, wolverine, lynx, muskrat).
- **The answer is gettable by the room.** Chopper, Hippos, Corsica, Boomerang, Shire, Guinness, Kirby, Doom, Bonnie and Clyde, Scarecrow, Montana, Obsidian. Obscure answers are rare and usually a person whose name is the hinge (Coco Crisp #147, Ryan Howard #281).
- When it's a person, it's someone the bar can say out loud: Pedro Martinez, Jack Nicholson, Tina Fey, Phil Collins, Bob Ross.

### Where the obscurity lives
**In the road, never the destination.** Predator is familiar; "rendezvous at the chopper" plus the WW2 motorcycle-stripping etymology is the deep cut (#56). Everybody knows Die Hard; nobody knew Akagi was the Pearl Harbor carrier (#420). Everybody knows Kirby; nobody knew he's named after Jack Kirby (#236). The pleasure is "I knew the answer and I did not know THAT about it." This is the single most important rule for the fact-hunter: **a fact only counts if it resolves to a word the room already owns.**

### Structure (2–3 sentences, read aloud)
1. **Hook** — optional, often a quote/sound that is itself a hint: "Stayin alive, stayin alive!" → pulsar (#69); "I don't like sand…" → Corsica (#71); "Leedle Leedle Leedle Lee!" → Flying Dutchman (#364); "Whistle whistle whistle" → Steamboat (#344); "Ow! My Patella!" → Wounded Knee (#365); "97X, Bam!" → Hoffman (#852); "Th-th-th-the first supermarket" → Piggly Wiggly (#1621); "Ok so, I'm never looking at Billy Joel album covers ever again…" → The Stranger (#2117); "Get a load of this!" → Sonic/Robotnik (#2623); "Arg!!!" → Plunder (#2626). The joke is the hint.
2. **Setup** — the fact, often anchored to "this past week," "legend states," or "according to [listicle]."
3. **Ask + bridge** — "what [category], that/which [second clue from another world]?"
4. **Wink** — trailing ellipsis, a pun, a family aside, a Michigan aside. "…PTTTTNNNNNNGGGGG!" (#188). "Giddyap, bullseye!" (#118). "For the record, I think the nickname Spike would be more appropriate." (#1154). "Ha, wimps." (#2052).

### How bridges actually work
A bridge is a **second doorway to the same answer from a different fandom**, not a second fact about the same subject. Stacking is the norm:
- Fleetwood = Elvis's Cadillac + half of an 80s band + a top-3 golfer (#198).
- Marley = Scrooge's partner + a Labrador + a reggae legend (#555).
- White Horse = oldest tavern + Stapleton song + Gandalf's ride (#629).
- Aurora = Illinois city + Colorado city + Roman goddess + Sleeping Beauty (#513).
- Mad Hatter = Carroll + Theophilus Carter + Disneyland storefront + Batman villain (#1005).
- Lighthouse = band name + Egypt + "Big Red" in Holland MI (#2054).
- Demi = Starbucks cup + demigods + Demi Moore (#2053).
- Scarecrow = Nagoro dolls + Wizard of Oz + Batman (#2625).
The bridge is frequently a **pun on the word itself** (Squib, Caper, Plum, Spurs, Parlay, Lead, Cannonball, Van Dyke). A bridge that is just "the movie he was also in" is not a bridge in Ben's grammar.

### Recurring forms
- **Lyric scenario:** "If you're Tracy Chapman and you happen to have a Fast Car, you work at what kind of establishment?" (#143). Also #264, #266, #308, #453, #536, #631, #1199, #1359, #1560, #1588, #1719, #1731, #1870, #1931, #2630. Second person, present tense.
- **"Scenario:" / "Picture this…"** — #266, #673, #882, #1152, #1379.
- **"If you were to take X and add Y"** word-construction — #312 Ganondorf, #371, #648 Shorts, #942 Yo-yo.
- **"Name either / for a bonus 5 / 5 points each"** — he likes giving two outs (#182, #585, #902, #1403, #1674, #1757, #1765, #1818).
- **"Heard here / seen here"** — audio/visual props used constantly (#222, #259, #491, #667, #755, #881, #900, #1142, #1300, #1378, #1382, #1714, #1766, #1835, #1878, #1906). A fact with a natural clip or photo is worth more.
- **Topical anchor** — 15–20% of regular questions open with a death, a release, an award show, a sports event, or a viral moment from that week (#196, #345, #517, #575–#588, #934, #1504, #1526, #1611, #1635, #2050, #2062, #2635–#2640). Deaths get a whole mini-round.

## 3. Bonus questions — how they differ

Two shapes, both harder than regular:

**(a) "For ten points each, name the five…" lists** (roughly half the bonus file): #290, #386, #426, #590, #662, #730, #752, #817, #838, #861, #885, #908, #933, #970, #996, #1018, #1038, #1060, #1081, #1123, #1167, #1188, #1210, #1234, #1259, #1281, #1307, #1327, #1349, #1417, #1437, #1477, #1497, #1498, #1521, #1564, #1584, #1606, #1649, #1669, #1686. These are listicle-derived with a **constraint twist**: two-word-title musicals (#1669), non-doll Toy HOF inductees (#1281), Big-4 nicknames not ending in S (#1018), capitals sharing two letters (#1167), four-letter countries (#996), symmetrical NFL logos (#1798), logos with faces (#1497), bands with "&" on the Walk of Fame (#1417), supergroups (#730), non-Disney animated franchises (#1259). The twist is the wordplay; the list is the work.

**(b) Hard single-answer with a longer, straighter story.** This is where **history** lives: Strangites on Beaver Island (#73), Wilmer McLean (#214), Andras Toma (#398), Mansa Musa (#483), Shanghai tunnels (#331), Library of Alexandria (#352), Harpers Ferry (#189), Gettysburg guide exam (#860), Sinatra's FBI file (#611), Reagan calling the called shot (#636), Smokey Bear's ZIP (#1080), Belushi's triple crown (#1122), Edith Head (#1809), Jimmy Stewart's rank (#1627), Arc of the Covenant guardian (#1605). The answer can be genuinely obscure (Lillehammer, Alan Arkin, Roberta Flack, Travis Tritt). Bridges still exist but lighter: Lillehammer/Luke Dunphy (#313), Budapest/Avengers (#398), Timbuktu/Shawn (#483), Lethal Weapon/Donner (#568).

Differences from regular: longer, fewer jokes, more "only/first/most" superlatives, list-shaped or history-shaped, and the obscurity is allowed to sit in the answer. **This is the one place a fact-hunter can pitch a straight history fact** — but it still wants a superlative or a constraint, not a lawsuit.

## 4. Shiny — formats, DNA, great vs flat

### Frequency (from the file)

## 6. PYL boards — what a real board is

### What separates them, bluntly
- **USED = a gettable word with a second doorway.** KILLED = a fact about a person, a court, or a debunked legend, whose answer nobody says at a bar (Phil LaMarr, Lee Seiler, Jorge Sandoval, Elena Bodnar).
- **The topics were fine.** Pulp Fiction, Toy Story, Grease, Empire, Se7en, Pixar, CIA, Detroit, Eagles — all his wells. The **angle** was wrong: trivia *about the production/legal history of* the fandom instead of a deep cut *inside* the fandom that resolves to a word.
- **Bridges are being written as filmographies.** "bridges=['Tootsie', 'Dog Day Afternoon']" is a list of movies the man was in. A Ben bridge is a pun, a name-collision, a Michigan tie, or a lyric. Renegade's bridges (Jeep / Styx / Jay-Z) are the template and it got used.
- **"Surprising" ≠ "cool."** Lawsuits are surprising. Nobody in a bar cheers a lawsuit. Cool = "wait, Kirby is named after JACK Kirby?"
- **The hunter isn't checking the bank** before delivering (Hootie, STP, Frank Oz #686).
- **The hunter's categories are the wrong axis.** "Records/Onlys/Firsts/Misc," "Film/TV," era tags — Ben's axis is *fandom × hinge type*. He said it himself: "fandoms are the goals here."
- **KEPT-but-not-used tells you the same thing.** Frank Oz (Yoda + Miss Piggy = name collision), UMich moon club (Michigan + space), Willie Mays every-inning (only + ghost-runner), Coco Chanel "Westminster" (codename hinge), Duracell bunny (mascot reversal), 555/Bruce Almighty (Jim Carrey well), Low Rider (Dazed and Confused + George Lopez). Every keep has a doorway.

### The used rate says the shape is wrong, not the subjects
11/600 with topics squarely in his wells means the generator is producing the right nouns in the wrong grammar. Fix the grammar (answer-first, hinge-required, legend-preserving, bank-checked) and the hit rate moves; adding more "surprising" facts will not.

### 8.6 The one-page brief every hunter and the grader should get (proposed text for `taste-profile.md` §0)
> **A Ben fact = a word the bar already owns, reached by a road it hasn't walked, with a second door.**
> 1. **Answer first.** Write the answer on line 1. If the table can't say it without being told, stop.
> 2. **Subject is a fandom, not a topic.** The Office, Star Wars, Disney parks, comics, Potter/LOTR/GoT, Pokémon/Nintendo, Nicktoons, dad rock, country, 2000s pop, Tigers/MSU/"the four major sports," Frat Pack comedies, cryptids/haunted/true crime, myth, Michigan/Saginaw, toys, fast food, booze. History, science, geography, etymology only arrive as the far side of a bridge.
> 3. **The hinge is the fact.** A name that's secretly something else; a real thing that leaked into fiction or vice versa; an almost-happened; somebody did the math; an only/first/last; a word origin with a pop twin. "Also voiced X" is a résumé. A lawsuit is a lawsuit. "Actually that's false" is a corpse.
> 4. **Bridges are doors, typed.** `name:` lives in another universe · `line:` a quote/lyric/SFX · `ask:` a character who'd know · `mi:` Michigan/family · `hook:` a cold-open that's secretly a clue. Filmographies are not bridges.
> 5. **Keep the legend.** "Legend states…" is a feature. Tag doubt `[disputed]`; never lead with the debunk.
> 6. **Gross is fine, grim is not.** Sloths, bat bombs, Uranus MO: yes. Murder trials, war wounds: no.
> 7. **Grep the bank for the hinge, not just the answer.** Hootie's name origin was already a swing item.
> 8. **Shape it like his notes page.** Answer, then 2–5 fragments ≤15 words, then hook/media if any, then the five-word shout.
